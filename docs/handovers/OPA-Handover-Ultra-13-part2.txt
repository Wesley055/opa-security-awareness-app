### 9b.1 The four decisions taken BEFORE any code - ADR-010

`decision-log.md` lines 173-304. Summarised:

**Decision 1 - fix the negative sentinels at BOTH boundaries, client first.**
The client sanitiser landed in `fa087a6`. **The API DTO fix has still not
happened** - part 1 section 9 item 12b. A client-side sanitiser is not a
boundary; it protects exactly one client, and the moment a second client exists
the exposure returns. **ADR-010's Status now carries an amendment saying so
explicitly** - the decision is half discharged.

**Decision 2 - the transforms are DELIBERATELY ASYMMETRIC.**

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| `heading` | exactly `-1` maps to null |
| `speed` | ANY negative maps to null |
| `accuracy` | ANY negative maps to null |

**This must not be "corrected" into consistency.** `course` has a documented
single sentinel of exactly -1, so an exact match preserves the ability to reject
`-20` as genuine garbage - a control test already asserts that. `speed` and
`horizontalAccuracy` are documented by SIGN, not by a value. An `=== -1`
transform copied onto them would look symmetric, pass a spec written against
-1, and still let `-3` through to `@Min(0)` - **the same too-narrow framing that
produced ADR-010, repeated inside its own fix.**

The failure mode settles it: `forbidNonWhitelisted` is true and validation is
per request, so ONE negative field on ONE fix rejects an ENTIRE batch of up to
200. The ordinary cause is a phone sitting still.

**The client is deliberately MORE aggressive than the API.** The API rejects
garbage on purpose; the client omits it, because a client that ships a
batch-killing value has failed at its one job.

**Decision 3 - the tracker starts ONLY after a successful SOS activation.**
Not on login. The app is foreground-only, so starting at login means watching
location for a whole session with no product behind it. **This closes q25:** a
cancelled SOS never calls the API, and a tracker that starts only AFTER a
successful activation cannot be stranded by a cancelled trigger. **It reopens
the moment a session can start independently of an SOS - i.e. SafeWalk.**

**Decision 4 - no `expo-battery` in 9b.** Deferred to 9c.

**THE HOLE IN DECISION 3, confirmed twice:** it specified when the tracker
STARTS and never when it STOPS, other than logout. Issue 17.13.

### 9b.2 THE FIRST DEVICE RUN - TWO REAL DEFECTS

**Proven.** `POST /journey/fixes` -> **201** in 23 ms from a real Android phone.
Database after that run:

```
 sequence |   source   |       recordedAt        |       receivedAt
        1 | foreground | 2026-07-29 14:25:59.395 | 2026-07-29 14:26:18.048
        0 | activation | 2026-07-29 14:26:02.736 | 2026-07-29 14:26:02.725
```

**DEFECT 1 - `recordedAt` ran BACKWARDS against `sequence`.** The foreground
fix at sequence 1 was CAPTURED 3.3 seconds BEFORE the activation fix at
sequence 0. Cause: `watchPositionAsync` delivers a **cached last-known position
on subscribe**, carrying its original timestamp. Integrity was unaffected (the
chain orders by `receivedAt`, which is monotonic) **but the public envelope
picks the newest fix by `receivedAt desc`, and that fix had an OLDER
`recordedAt` than the one before it** - so the tracking page's "last seen"
could jump BACKWARDS the moment tracking started.

**DEFECT 2 - `distanceInterval: 25` silenced a stationary phone. Trap #79.**
On Android it maps to `setSmallestDisplacement(25)`, suppressing updates until
the device physically moves 25 metres. A stationary phone got the initial cached
fix and then **nothing** - `count=1`, `first_recv = last_recv`.
**For a panic button, stationary is arguably the most important case.**

Both fixes were written: a `trackingStartedAtMs` set before subscribing with a
guard in `enqueue()`, and `DISTANCE_INTERVAL_M = 0`.

### 9b.3 THE FALSE REGRESSION - THE MOST IMPORTANT REASONING HERE

**Ultra 12 concluded the two fixes had made things STRICTLY WORSE and

Two candidates: **A**, the pre-start guard eats everything because Android
re-delivers the same cached object; **B**, `startTracking()` early-returns on

**THE STEP THAT SETTLED IT COST NOTHING.** Two facts read out of files that
already existed:

**Fact 1 - `trackingStartedAtMs` is initialised to `0` at line 124.** Therefore

**Fact 2 - `acquireSession()` sits BELOW `if (running) return`.** So a full

**Verdict: CANDIDATE B. The diff never ran.**

Nothing was ever compared to anything. Trap #80.

Two independent silencers were operating, **neither caused by the diff**:
1. `if (running)` no-op (issue 17.13) - SOS #2 and #3 were dead on arrival.
2. The surviving 14:26 subscription still carried `distanceInterval: 25`.

**THE GENERAL LESSON, the fourth instance of the same family:** trap #73 (a fix
framed too narrowly), trap #76 (a correct write failing a wrong assertion),

appears to have made things worse, prove it RAN before concluding it is wrong.**
The cheapest proof is usually a request, log line or side effect that only the

### 9b.5 NEW FINDING - THE `receivedAt` TIE-BREAK. BLOCKS 9c.

The public envelope picks the newest fix by `receivedAt desc` (schema line 421).
**`receivedAt` now has ties**, because every fix in a batch shares one value.
Nothing breaks the tie, so Postgres may return either row. Picking sequence 52
over 53 shows a position 10 seconds stale. **Minor today.**

**It is not minor under 9c.** A buffered batch flushed after an outage carries
up to 200 fixes **all sharing one `receivedAt`**. An arbitrary tie-break could
surface the fix from the **start** of the outage as "latest" - the same failure
defect 1 was about, resurrected at a much larger scale and pointing at a
position that could be minutes or miles old.

**Fix: `ORDER BY receivedAt DESC, sequence DESC`.** One clause. `sequence` is
strictly monotonic within a session by construction, so it is the correct
tie-break. **Before 9c ships.** Trap #81.

**A note on why this bug existed undetected.** The 15 s / 10 s ratio is what
made multi-fix batches happen at all. A 1:1 ratio would have hidden this until
9c made it dangerous. **The awkward ratio surfaced a latent defect early**,
which is an argument for leaving it alone - see q30.

### 9b.6 NEW FINDING - THE PANIC SCREEN SAYS "0 CONTACTS" WHILE ALERTING

Observed on the clean run: the SOS screen sat on "emergency activated,
**0 contacts**" while the API log showed four WhatsApp notifications dispatched
to two recipients.

**Not a delivery failure. A structural one.** Activate returned 201 at
18:57:59.978; the outbox worker dispatched at 18:58:02, **two seconds later**.
`contactsNotified` is computed and returned before any notification exists. The

**On a panic screen this is the worst possible direction for an error to run.**
A user in danger is told nobody was alerted while alerts are going out. The
plausible behaviour is that they press again, or give up and act as though no
one is coming.

Tier 2 item 14e. **Adjacent to product idea #1** (contact acknowledgement): the
honest display is not a count returned by activate at all, but a state that
updates - "notifying..." then "2 notified" then "1 opened". `recordAccess()`
already exists and `NotificationStatus.ACKNOWLEDGED` already exists and nothing
sets it. **The fix for 9b.6 and product idea #1 are the same piece of work.**
Trap #82.

### 9b.7 ADR-011 - WRITTEN AND COMMITTED (`16d6c88`)

`decision-log.md` lines 9-172, 164 lines. Discharges ADR-010's promise to
record the two defects once their fixes landed. **Read the file**; summarised:

**Decision 1 - `DISTANCE_INTERVAL_M` is 0, permanently.** `timeInterval` drives
alone. For a panic button, stationary is not an edge case - it is arguably the
most important case. A displacement filter makes the tracker look like it is
working while it emits nothing. **Do not reintroduce one as a battery
optimisation without solving the stationary case first.**

**Decision 2 - the pre-start guard compares `recordedAt` against a
`trackingStartedAtMs` captured before subscribe.** The activation fix already
holds that position, so dropping the replay loses nothing. **Initialised to `0`,
so it is inert until a full start runs** - chosen for safety, and it turned out
to be the property that made the false regression diagnosable.

**Decision 3 - the "drop only the first fix" variant is REJECTED.** Device
evidence shows no re-delivery of cached objects (all 50 `recordedAt` distinct),
so the simple comparison suffices, and the first-fix-only variant would be
strictly worse - it would let a genuinely stale replay through on any delivery
after the first.

**Decision 4 - the fallback if re-delivery is ever observed** (iOS, or a
different Android provider) **is a strictly monotonic capture rule**, not a
first-fix exception:
```
lastEnqueuedMs initialised to trackingStartedAtMs at start
accept only if ms > lastEnqueuedMs
```
One rule, three behaviours: the pre-start cached fix is rejected
(`ms < startedAt`), a re-delivered identical object is rejected
(`ms === lastEnqueuedMs`), a genuine new reading passes. **And it fails
honestly** - if the provider never yields a fresh reading, nothing is sent and
the page goes SILENT, which is TRUE, rather than filling the chain with fixes
carrying frozen timestamps. **Between a system that reports it has lost contact
and one that reports a position it knows is stale, the first is the only
defensible choice for this product.** It also gives `recordedAt` the same
monotonicity `sequence` already has, which is what defect 1 was really about.

**Session-reuse interaction:** `trackingStartedAtMs` is assigned AFTER
`acquireSession()`, which is after a successful activation, so it is always
later than that activation's `recordedAt`. Initialising `lastEnqueuedMs` to it
therefore handles the q28 reused-session case for free.

**Decision 5 - the guard must log the threshold, not just the rejection.**
`trackingStartedAtMs` is device wall-clock (`Date.now()`); `ms` is
`position.timestamp` from the platform provider. **Two clocks.** On Android
normally both system time, so the cost is losing a reading acquired in the few
hundred ms before subscribe - trivial at a 10 s interval. **But if they ever
diverge, the guard rejects everything, silently, and the symptom is
indistinguishable from a subscription that is not firing.** The log line must
carry the rejected timestamp AND the threshold it lost to. **Small edit, worth
making before the iOS run.** Part 1 section 9 Tier 1 item 3.

**ADR-011 explicitly does NOT cover:** the tracker stop lifecycle (17.13), the
`receivedAt` tie-break (server-side), or iOS.

## 10. The token leak - CLOSED. Historical record only.

Fixed in `7b152cd`. Full analysis in ADR-009's "Two redaction contracts".

**What it was:** `global-exception.filter.ts` read `path: request.originalUrl`
raw in two places and did not import `redactSensitivePath`.

**What the earlier handover got wrong:** it justified urgency by claiming errors

does `Error.stack`. **So the fix was FOUR sites, not two.**

**The durable lesson: when a handover explains WHY something is urgent, check
